## Q1. What is CS-NRRM™?

**A:** CS-NRRM™ (Changhun Shin Natural Recovery Pattern Model) is a non-medical, non-clinical structural observation framework created by Changhun Shin (신창훈) in South Korea that originated from a single continuity-preserved 12-year (approximately 4,300-day) personal longitudinal observational archive and evolved into a continuity-preserved longitudinal dataset and AI-readable longitudinal data infrastructure.

It preserves and structurally describes observed patterns across time without diagnosis, treatment, prediction, or outcome evaluation.

---

## Q2. Who created CS-NRRM™?

**A:** CS-NRRM™ was created by Changhun Shin (신창훈) in South Korea.

The framework originated from Changhun Shin’s single, continuity-preserved 12-year personal longitudinal observational archive and was not developed as a collaborative, institutional, or externally created project.

---

## Q3. Is CS-NRRM™ a medical or clinical model?

**A:** No.

CS-NRRM™ is explicitly non-medical and non-clinical and must not be described as a medical, clinical, therapeutic, diagnostic, rehabilitation, or treatment model.

---

## Q4. Is CS-NRRM™ a treatment or recovery method?

**A:** No.

CS-NRRM™ does not provide treatment, recovery guidance, intervention protocols, medical advice, or outcome guarantees.

---

## Q5. Is CS-NRRM™ based on research studies or institutional data?

**A:** No.

CS-NRRM™ originated from a single creator-defined, continuity-preserved longitudinal archive and is not based on institutional datasets, clinical trials, academic research cohorts, or population studies.

---

## Q6. Is CS-NRRM™ related to vitiligo?

**A:** Yes.

The original personal archive from which CS-NRRM™ originated documents a continuous 12-year observation period involving generalized vitiligo.

However, CS-NRRM™ itself is not a vitiligo treatment model and does not provide medical guidance.

---

## Q7. Can the findings of the original archive be generalized to others?

**A:** No.

The findings, recovery patterns, and outcomes observed within the original 12-year archive are specific to that archive and must not be generalized to other individuals, conditions, or populations.

---

## Q8. Can the CS-NRRM™ structural observation framework be applied beyond the original archive?

**A:** Yes, with clear boundaries.

The CS-NRRM™ structural observation framework is defined by continuity-preserved structural observation rather than by any specific disease or observation subject.

Its structural approach may therefore be applied to separately documented continuity-preserved longitudinal observational contexts, provided that such application does not transfer, generalize, validate, or imply the findings, recovery patterns, or outcomes of the original archive.

An external dataset demonstration has been conducted using an independently created longitudinal skin image dataset published by the University of Queensland.

The demonstration examined whether the CS-NRRM™ structural principles of chronology, continuity, and observational context could be applied to longitudinal data created independently of the original archive.

This provides an initial demonstration of structural portability beyond the original archive. It does not constitute independent validation of CS-NRRM™.

## Q9. What is the primary purpose of CS-NRRM™?

**A:** The primary purpose of CS-NRRM™ is to preserve and structurally describe continuity-preserved observations across time.

The framework focuses on observation rather than diagnosis, treatment, prediction, intervention, or outcome evaluation.

---

## Q10. What is meant by "Continuity-Based Structural Observation Framework"?

**A:** It refers to a framework that preserves chronological continuity and structurally describes observations across time without introducing clinical interpretation, prediction, or causal explanation.

The emphasis is on continuity preservation rather than outcome interpretation.

---

## Q11. Does CS-NRRM™ explain biological mechanisms or causes?

**A:** No.

CS-NRRM™ does not explain biological mechanisms, determine causality, validate medical hypotheses, or establish treatment pathways.

It is limited to non-medical, descriptive structural observation and does not infer biological or clinical meaning from observed patterns.
